# Personalization and interview workflow

## When the interview is required

Run this workflow for a new Career Context file, a major restructuring, or a material change in professional direction. For a small factual update, ask only the questions needed by the affected entry.

The interview is a discovery process, not a form to complete mechanically. Ask in short rounds, synthesize what was learned, and let later questions respond to earlier answers.

## Discovery sequence

1. Ask what a future agent should understand after reading the file.
2. Ask which two or three experiences, projects, or decisions define the person most strongly now.
3. Ask what direction the person wants to pursue over the next several years and which paths should remain open.
4. Ask which subjects are established strengths, emerging capabilities, active learning areas, and future targets.
5. Ask which subjects appear in the history but should remain supporting means rather than the person's future identity.
6. Ask which claims would feel exaggerated, misleading, outdated, or personally unwanted even if adjacent facts are true.
7. Ask the user to rank major content groups, such as experience, research, independent projects, education, achievements, community work, and affiliations.
8. Ask about practical targeting constraints only when relevant: roles, location, work mode, relocation, availability, and exclusions.

Do not ask every question in one large questionnaire. Ask the highest-leverage questions first, summarize the emerging model, and resolve only the ambiguities that would change structure or positioning.

## Positioning synthesis

Before a large rewrite, return a concise synthesis for confirmation:

- current evidence-backed identity
- long-term direction and paths kept open
- defining evidence to emphasize
- supporting foundations and how they serve the objective
- capabilities that are emerging or targeted
- identities and claims to avoid
- proposed body order and relative depth

Revise the synthesis until it reflects the user's intent. Do not draft polished positioning from inferred ambition alone.

## Entry interview

For each defining role, research item, or project, establish:

- **Purpose:** Why did the work exist and what problem mattered?
- **Ownership:** What did the person personally decide, build, test, research, or maintain?
- **Approach:** Why was this approach selected and which trade-offs mattered?
- **Evidence:** What was delivered, observed, learned, or achieved?
- **Interpretation:** Why does the result matter, including negative or bounded findings?
- **Relevance:** How does the work support the person's direction, when the connection is not self-evident?
- **Boundary:** What must not be inferred from the entry?

Not every label must appear in the final file. Use only the fields needed to make the entry accurate and useful.

## Claim-state model

Use precise states in notes, evidence lines, or boundaries when ambiguity could cause overclaiming:

- verified employment or formal role
- completed implementation or delivered artifact
- completed research or documented evaluation
- proposed or analyzed architecture
- practical exposure
- active learning
- community membership or affiliation
- evidenced contribution
- target development area
- unsupported claim

Membership is not contribution. Affiliation is not employment. A proposal is not an implementation. Coursework exposure is not mature professional expertise. A future target is not current capability.

## Review loop

During a major rewrite, review one defining entry at a time. Confirm that purpose, ownership, implementation depth, results, metrics, relevance, and boundaries have the right balance. Then complete a whole-file pass for repetition, hierarchy, chronology, and claim consistency.
